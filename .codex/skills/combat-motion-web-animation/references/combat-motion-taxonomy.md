# Combat Motion Taxonomy

Use a shared vocabulary before designing or reviewing motion.

## Core Phases

- `windup`: the readable preparation before force is released
- `travel`: attacker movement, projectile travel, or energy transfer
- `hit-stop`: the intentional pause or slowdown on contact
- `impact`: the frame or short burst that sells force
- `resolution`: health change, destroy state, or effect outcome becoming visible
- `recovery`: return to stable board state

## Feedback Layers

- `primary motion`: the attacker, projectile, or target actually moves
- `impact flash`: white or colored flash at the hit frame
- `shape accent`: slash arc, ring, spike, burst, or directional streak
- `screen feedback`: shake, zoom, or global flash
- `target reaction`: recoil, squash, shrink, crack, or dissolve
- `residuals`: smoke, embers, trails, or lingering glow

Each layer must have a reason. If a layer does not improve readability or force, cut it.

## Interaction Patterns

## Combat Attack

- Goal: feel decisive and readable
- Usually needs: windup, travel, hit-stop, impact, recovery
- Common failure: too much travel, weak impact, no recoil

## Ability Cast

- Goal: communicate charging and release
- Usually needs: anticipation, charge buildup, release burst, payload travel, resolution
- Common failure: long buildup with weak payoff

## Destroy Resolution

- Goal: make removal feel final without delaying the game too much
- Usually needs: target callout, fatal impact, vanish or collapse, board settle
- Common failure: target disappears without a convincing fatal moment

## Card Entry

- Goal: focus attention and settle quickly
- Usually needs: spawn, emphasis, settle
- Common failure: entry steals attention from combat-critical motion

## Anti-Patterns

- Long continuous movement without a single clear hit frame
- Multiple flashes of equal strength with no focal point
- Particle spam that hides the target or board state
- Screen shake without impact alignment
- Timing that delays state resolution after the player already understands the result
